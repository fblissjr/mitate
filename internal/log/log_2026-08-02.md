The finding that reorders what follows: **validation clusters where a mistake is

## Later the same day — R5.3, `build.js check` (0.16.67)

Picked up the item the block above names as next. Shipped four of its six
work-list items, blocked one on the prime directive, and found the sixth does not
exist.

**The routing question came first and decided everything else.** The work-list
needs `SHOTS[].at`, `subject`, `focus`, `size`, `SUBJECTS` keys and the `SIZES`
ladder. Only `{t, cutEnd}` of that is on `window`, and SUBJECTS/SIZES are on no
window at all. Three options: widen the contract across every carrier, reach into
scene internals through `page.evaluate` (a second probe exception), or read the
source text. Took the third. It drives nothing, so the prime directive's clause
does not bind it; it knows only kit-owned table names, never a film's own
identifiers, which is the property the clause exists to protect. And it works on
a scene too broken to load — which matters, because a shot naming a nonexistent
beat throws inside `beatAt` at load, so a contract reader would have nothing to
inspect on exactly the input that most needs checking.

**Then it condemned a shipped film on its first run, exactly as predicted.**
"Union shots use only wide rungs" fired on `bear-and-bees.html`'s two-shot —
which is deliberate, annotated in place, and asks for `MS` on a pair with
`anchor:.45`. Reading the solver settled it: an explicit `anchor` overrides the
rung's own, so that shot supplies precisely the landmark a union box lacks. The
narrowed rule is silent across the whole corpus. Without the narrowing the verb's
first output would have been a false accusation against a correct example, which
is this repo's oldest check-authoring failure and it took ten minutes to walk
into again.

**The corpus fixture is a thinner positive control than expected, and that is
information.** `after-hours.html` is clean against every on-spec check. Its
declarative defects are #11 (declared extent, the blocked one) and #10b (repeated
framing), so a directive-respecting checker reaches exactly one of them. Added
the repeated-framing warning for that reason and said so — it is beyond the
work-list, decidable from the table, and the only thing on this fixture a text
pass can see. It also re-measures 10b: **four** identical framings, not the five
the prototype's squint strip reported. The fifth differs by `elev` alone. The eye
saw a repeat; the table sees a different shot.

**Mutation-tested all fourteen new arms** — each check neutralised in a mirrored
