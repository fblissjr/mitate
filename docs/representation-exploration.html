Where data stops and code starts

Drafted 2026-08-02 from the session draft position, the session brief, references/breakdown.md, and the authored surface of plugin/skills/mitate/examples/menagerie.html. Revised the same day after an alignment audit against VISION.md, docs/plan.md, and the site's strategic copy. Boxes marked "audit note" show where that audit changed a claim. Figures carry the command or definition that derives them; prefer re-running to citing.

Exploration, not doctrine. This page argues one side of the design session. It settles nothing on its own — and the session has now run: the decision was recorded on 2026-08-02 in docs/representation.md, which wins wherever the two disagree. The page stays as the argument that went in, with audit notes where checking changed a claim. The live phases and gates are doctrine and live in docs/plan.md's representation track (REP0–REP6); the walkthrough below explains them and points there, it does not own them.

What was decided, and why

The record is docs/representation.md's decision section (2026-08-02, owner). This is the same seven points read with their reasons — the why is this page's job, the record is that file's:

  1. The boundary is decidability, with typed holes. Data is what you can decide without running the scene; every place decidability ends is a named function with a declared signature. Why: it is the only line that both blesses the two shipped patterns that already work (SUBJECTS.pos, matFor) and gives a validator something it can enumerate — the holes are a complete list of where checking ends, instead of checking ending ambiently, everywhere.
  2. The line moves by cost-scaled triggers, not a reframe. Why: a single trigger for every form is the defect plan.md's promotion table was built to fix, and the extents case proves the point — no film was ever blocked by a wrong extent; films were reliably wrong, which is why the trigger now says both. Adopted with plan.md's own caveat attached: the triggers are unfireable without a count until REP5 instruments the counting.
  3. Three kinds, not two: authored data, derived data, code. Why: the repo already forbids hand-writing BEAT{} and TOTAL, and a physics bake will make the never-authored, always-regenerable kind the largest of the three. Naming it now is what prevents re-litigating the boundary the day the bake lands.
  4. Extents derive from construction, with a named intent channel. Why: the recorded evidence (hand-declared wrong three of five; ledger count 6) says declaration is the failure mode — and deriving makes the defect class unrepresentable, the strongest fix this repo recognizes, while the named-term rule keeps authorial intent visible instead of laundering it into a derived number.
  5. The kit is emitted, not replicated. Why: the measured composition — declaration literals are 0.4% of one carrier's fenced surface — says the parity tax is the cost of replicating machinery, so the cure is emission, not schemas. The byte-identity gate is what makes the change safe: nothing may differ until everything provably matches.
  6. The harness stays in the JS family. Why: every language boundary is a reimplementation boundary, and the open findings are the measured cost of reimplementation inside one language. TypeScript at the seams gets decided in REP2 where the seams are written anyway; other languages enter only behind derived-data boundaries.
  7. Durable foundation. The engine, primitives, and declarative layers are the destination, not scaffolding models outgrow. Why it had to be decided: the site hedged the opposite, and how hard REP1 is worth pressing depends on which sentence is true.

The question, and the stakes

Where is the boundary between data and code in a mitate scene? Everything else in this design space is downstream of it: what the source artifact is, what the fenced blocks become, what a compiler validates, and how a migration runs.

Two measured costs make it worth deciding now. This page argues they are different problems:

  • Duplication. The parity run holds 5,704 fenced lines byte-identical across nine carriers (derived by smoke.js --parity-only, 2026-08-02; the surface grows with every example). Policing it costs a propagation tool, a bracket, a pre-commit hook, and a three-glob discipline.
  • Validation in the wrong place. Checks cluster where a mistake is unrepresentable: an unknown name fails a lookup. They are absent where a mistake is expensive: an extent that disagrees with its geometry passes silently. The static validator that exists, build.js check, reimplements kit semantics, and nothing compares the two models. Thirteen open findings share that single root cause (postmortem, 2026-08-02).

The claim

Data is the part of a scene you can decide without running it. Code is the part you must run. A function inside a table does not violate that line. It marks, with a type, exactly where decidability ends, and the schema around the hole makes the ending explicit instead of ambient.

A fact earns the data side when a second consumer uses that decidability: a validator, a solver, a retimer, a recorder, a stager. Every table that exists today passes that test. Nothing was made data for purity's sake, and nothing should be.

Pure literalsBEATS, FRAME, CONFIG, proportion vectors. Fully decidable; validated unevenly today.
Tables with resolutionSHOTS: literal rows, anchors resolved against BEATS. Still decidable, and check proves it.
Typed holesSUBJECTS.pos and matFor(part): declared signature, opaque body. Decidability ends here, visibly.
Closed formsbearXAt(t): named seams that data and code share, so two consumers cannot disagree.
Authored codebuildWorlds() and animate(), where most of a film's lines live. Reviewable, not decidable.
readable, validatable, diffable as fact
runnable; judged by the three review axes, not by a schema
Audit note. This test is weaker than VISION's. VISION asks whether a declaration can be read, validated, or rendered independently of the artifact that embeds it (VISION.md, "Where a declaration lives"). Decidability-without-execution says nothing about artifact independence: a table you can parse out of the HTML still lives only inside the HTML, and the SHOTS resolution interleaving shows how fragile that parsing is. The roadmap below now states what it does not deliver on this axis.

The foundational form: an invariant plus movement triggers

This is a long-horizon decision, so the thing worth recording is not an inventory of today's tables. Any list goes stale at the next capability. Record three commitments instead:

1 · The invariant

Everything on the data side is decidable and validatable without executing a scene. Every place decidability ends is a named, typed hole, never ambient code mixed through the declarations.

2 · The movement triggers

The line advances by triggers that scale with the cost of the form, per the plan's promotion table: a craft rule on the second instance, a primitive on a second consumer, a new fence only on a third, and a vocabulary change when a film is blocked saying something the tables cannot say, or is not blocked but reliably wrong. Each migration must hand a named second consumer a decision it could not previously make.

3 · The permanent residue

Some code never migrates, by design. The hand-rolled case is not a backlog of not-yet-data. It is half the reason primitives exist (constraint 4).

Audit note. The first version of commitment 2 was a single gate: advance only on a pattern-ledger recurrence count. docs/plan.md's promotion section names exactly that as the defect it fixed: "one trigger for four costs is the defect." Its cost table gives each layer its own trigger, and the vocabulary layer, which is the data side of this boundary, advances on blockedness, not on a count. Rewritten to match. The ledger still matters: the plan also concludes every trigger is unfireable without a count, which is what roadmap phase REP5 instruments.

Three kinds, not two

The two-sided line hides a third kind the repo already treats specially: things that are never authored and always regenerable. BEAT{} and TOTAL are forbidden to hand-write today. stage-films.sh derives the staged films. A future physics bake is the same kind at much larger volume.

authored data

Tables, vectors, declared intent. An author writes it, a validator checks it before render, and it owns the meaning.

derived data

Computed from authored data by a tool at build time: beat spans, durations, staged copies, bakes. Never hand-edited. Staleness is always checkable because the deriving command is named.

code

Construction and per-frame motion. Runnable, judged by review. The measured lessons live as comments beside the lines they constrain.

Derived data is small today and becomes the largest of the three the day anything is baked. A representation decision with no place for it gets re-litigated then.

Two axes, not one: the cost map

Cross data/code with kit versus film. Kit means fenced and byte-identical in every carrier; film means authored per scene. The two measured costs live in different cells:

datacode
kit SIZES, the cut-duration table (they ride inside fenced blocks) KERNEL · SOLVER · RIG · DRIVER · CHARACTER · CONTRACT · HTML
the parity tax lives here
film BEATS · STYLE · CONFIG · FRAME · SUBJECTS · SHOTS · proportion vectors
every validation gap lives here		 buildWorlds() add-ons · animate() · helpers (53 to 172 lines per shipped example, 701 in the corpus fixture; no static check, no parity, no reference covers any of it)

Consequence: duplication is not evidence that film content should become data. It is evidence that kit code should be emitted rather than replicated. A compiler over the kit row solves duplication (agenda items 2 and 3). Schemas over the film row solve validation (question 1 proper). Two separable programs; either can ship without the other.

Audit note, with the number that settles it. The first version asserted the split ("entirely kit code") without measuring it, and VISION reads the other way: its "Where a declaration lives" section attributes the parity tax to declarations not being independently readable. Measured 2026-08-02, on menagerie's seven fence regions (823 lines, classified line-by-line by a throwaway script): 3 lines are declaration literals, the SIZES ladder and the cut table, or 0.4%; 458 lines are machinery code (56%); 341 are comment-only (41%); 21 blank. The replicated surface is machinery and its comments, so the tax funds the emission program, not the schema program. VISION's deeper claim survives untouched: declarations still cannot be read, validated, or rendered independently of the artifact. The session should reconcile VISION's attribution paragraph with this composition; per repo rule that is a fact question, and the number wins arguments prose cannot.

How the line moves, and the one live fork

The move, illustrated: TRAVEL (hypothetical, deliberately not proposed now)

// The same move BEATS made, applied to the latent data in the closed forms:
const TRAVEL = { bear: {from: -16.5, to: -6.1, over: ['walk', .02, .92]} };

The moment this exists, a second consumer exists: check could decide statically that a subject's travel stays inside some shot's coverage for the whole film, which nothing can decide today. When it happens is a trigger question: as a vocabulary change, the plan says it fires when a film is blocked or reliably wrong, and the ledger's count is the evidence that a trigger fired. One film's travel pattern is neither, yet. This is the shape of every future migration: find the latent data, name the second consumer, watch the trigger.

The extent fork: decide it or defer it, but from the record

The layer's most expensive unvalidated fact is SUBJECTS.h/w/d versus the geometry it claims to describe. Both resolutions are half-present in the tree, and they pull the boundary in opposite directions:

A · Declare and compare

Extents are authored data. probe measures the built geometry, and a check compares the two.

for
  • Extents stay statically readable, so check can reason about framing without a browser
  • Stays inside probe's existing exception; acquires no new one
  • The declaration remains stated intent, and drift becomes detectable
against
  • The comparison needs the runtime; the check is not purely static
  • Two sources of truth, held in agreement by an instrument, forever

B · Derive and dissolve

Extents are computed from construction, as menagerie's h: bear.height+.3 partly does. The check dissolves because the declaration cannot be wrong.

for
  • The defect class becomes unrepresentable, the strongest kind of fix this repo recognizes
  • No instrument to run, no drift to police
against
  • Extents stop being statically readable, and check loses framing reasoning entirely
  • The fudge terms (+.3, d: 1.6) are intent that derivation cannot produce, so in practice this is a hybrid, and the hybrid needs a rule
Audit note. The first version presented this fork as evidence-free. It is not. docs/plan.md's promotion worked-examples record hand-declared extents wrong three times out of five, while the one framed from measured rig.height/centerX was right first try, filed as "evidence for a vocabulary change, measured extents replacing declared." The pattern-ledger holds declared-extents at count 6, cited as proof the trigger had long fired. The choice stays with the owner, because it turns on whether an extent is intent or consequence. But the recorded evidence leans derive and measure, and the session should start from that record, not from a blank fork.

Decisions and tradeoffs

D1 · Typed holes sit on the data side

This rejects both alternatives: pure-literal tables (demotes pos and matFor and buys no consumer anything) and functions-anywhere (dissolves decidability).

for
  • Blesses the two shipped patterns that already work
  • Keeps tracking shots for free, and material packs
  • Holes are enumerable, so a validator can list every point where decidability ends
against
  • A hole is opaque to every static instrument; each one added cedes surface
  • Needs discipline, or a check, that holes carry declared signatures

D2 · Cost-scaled movement triggers, not a one-time reframe

The line moves by the plan's trigger table: blockedness for vocabulary, consumer counts for primitives and fences, instances for craft. Not by a single architectural migration of geometry and motion into declarations.

for
  • Every migration lands with its consumer and its evidence attached
  • No speculative schema, so the "another copy of the code" failure cannot start
  • Matches how BEATS, SHOTS, and FRAME actually happened, and matches the plan's promotion model exactly
against
  • Honest counter: if the strategic aim is a deliberate leap, geometry as declarations the way SHOTS did cameras, trigger-gating is too slow and this decision is miscast as process
  • The triggers are unfireable without a count, and counting is manual today (REP5)

D3 · Derived data is a first-class kind

Named now, before a bake makes it the largest kind. The rule: never authored, always regenerable, deriving command named where the artifact lives.

for
  • The bake proposal slots in without re-litigating the boundary
  • Staleness is always checkable: recompute and compare
against
  • A third kind is more ontology to hold; the repo currently manages with per-artifact conventions

D4 · The kit is emitted, not replicated

Fenced blocks become build products from one canonical source. The shipped artifact is still one self-contained HTML file; invariant 1 is a delivery constraint, and emission satisfies it.

for
  • Retires the parity tax and its police apparatus: tool, bracket, hook, three-glob discipline
  • check and the kit read one source, so the thirteen-findings class dissolves instead of being bracketed
  • Comments survive: the canonical copy carries them once (constraint 5)
against
  • A compiler is a new load-bearing tool that needs its own controls, red first
  • git diff on an example stops showing the whole artifact, so reviewability of the emitted file must be deliberately preserved
  • The site's public bet said "the one scene file stays the source" and "diffs and versions like source." Emission broke source-equals-artifact as messaged, so the decision included site work — done in 0.17.0, inside REP1, as prescribed
  • Migration touches every carrier; sequencing risk is real (gate REP1)

D5 · Close the open bags

STYLE and CONFIG gain a declared key registry: kit-read keys with shapes and defaults, film-private keys explicitly marked. check warns on unknown keys.

for
  • A misspelled exposure stops rendering at the default and passing as a choice
  • Kit/film name collision, a recorded hazard, becomes detectable
  • Kit-read keys no template declares (flashWidth, cameraFloor) become reachable without reading kit source
against
  • A registry can drift from the code that reads the keys. It needs a derivation or a control, or it repeats the six-entry-registry failure

D6 · The extent fork

Open, but not blank: the plan's recorded evidence (three of five hand-declared extents wrong; ledger count 6) leans derive and measure. Recorded here so its absence from the decision list is visible rather than silent.

D7 · The harness stays in the JS family

Owner question, 2026-08-02: should the harness move off pure JavaScript, any language fair game? Position: no. Stay JS-family under bun, decide TypeScript at the seams during the emitter work, and admit other languages only behind derived-data boundaries.

for staying
  • The scene is JS by necessity, and both harness jobs are JS-adjacent: driving a browser through the window contract, and reading JS table literals out of scene source
  • Every language boundary is a reimplementation boundary. The thirteen findings happened with check and the kit in the same language; a second language makes REP2's shared-semantics fix impossible by construction
  • bun runs TypeScript natively, so types at the seams cost no build step. That is schema-where-things-connect expressed in the harness's own language
against / where it bends
  • Types that restate runtime validators are another copy of the code; types and validators need one home or they drift
  • The bake is a legitimate exception: simulation emits derived data, so its language is invisible behind the artifact. Wasm has spec-pinned float semantics where JS Math.sin is implementation-defined, which strengthens re-bake reproducibility, a question VISION already flags
  • Python, Go, or Rust for the harness proper: rejected. Playwright exists there, but every table read becomes a cross-language parse and the shared-semantics control dies

What this means now, and longer term

Now

  • Nothing authored changes hands immediately. Today's tables already satisfy the invariant, and today's holes already have signatures.
  • The thirteen open check findings get a strategy instead of a backlog: if D4 proceeds, the class dissolves; the comparison bracket (postmortem forward item 5) gets built only if D4 stalls. A trigger, not a preference.
  • The parity apparatus keeps running unchanged until REP1's gate is green. No check relaxes in anticipation.

Longer term

  • At bake time: a bake is derived data arriving into a slot that already exists. No boundary re-litigation.
  • As state widens ({t, baked}, {t, view}): schema at the seam again. The window contract stays thin and untouched.
  • At agent scale: the boundary doubles as the line between checkable by machine, cheaply, always, and reviewable by judgment, per film. The ratio of films to reviewers only grows. Machine checks raise the floor; looking stays the method, which is also how the site already frames the gate ("the lint can reject a scene; it can never approve one").

Roadmap walkthrough: how each phase is reached, and what closes its gate

The roadmap as argued 2026-08-02, relabeled REP0–REP6 to stop colliding with the restructure plan's R-gates. The live phase and gate definitions are doctrine and live in docs/plan.md's representation track — that section's wording is normative and is where MET gets stamped; this walkthrough explains the path into each phase and the mechanics of closing it.

What closes a gate, uniformly: a demonstration, never an assertion. Each gate names a condition a command output or a bracket run can exhibit — the closure is the red-then-green pair (the control shown failing on the bad case before passing on the real one), cited by run in the commit that claims it. Prose saying "done" closes nothing; this repo's record shows "measured" written without a control is how gates rot. Strangler-shaped throughout: every step keeps invariant 1 (one self-contained HTML file that opens from disk) and both workflows green, and no check relaxes in anticipation of a later phase.

REP0

Decide and record

The session reaches, or explicitly defers, the boundary decision and the extent fork. The outcome lands in docs/representation.md's decision section in the owner's words: the boundary as drawn, the cases it cannot hold, what was rejected and why, what would make it wrong.

done if: the decision section is non-empty, or the deferral is recorded with its trigger. Owner's call throughout; this page is one input. met: 2026-08-02 — the decision section is written; the seven points it records are listed there, not restated here.

REP1

Single-source the kit (the emitter)

The fenced blocks move to one canonical home, and a build step assembles scenes from it. The first gate is deliberately zero-behavior-change: the emitter must reproduce all nine current carriers byte-identically before anything may differ. Then carriers flip from hand-maintained to derived, and the parity check inverts, from "nine copies agree with each other" to "every carrier matches emitter output," before eventually becoming a bracket over the emitter itself. This phase also owned the messaging change: the site's "the one scene file stays the source" stopped being true here, and 0.17.0 reworded it in the same release, as prescribed — the wording rode inside the phase, not after it.

path in: open now — the spike proved feasibility, so the work starts at the canonical layout (where the one copy of each fence lives, and how it keeps its comments), then the emitter, then its bracket proven red on a mutated fence before the first carrier flips to derived. The site wording change rides inside this phase, not after it.

done if: emission reproduces every carrier byte-for-byte, making the parity green a corollary instead of a discipline, and --parity-fix's job shrinks to "regenerate." refuted if: carriers need per-film divergence inside fences, which would mean the fences were never really kit and the cost map above is miscarved.

REP2

One source for semantics: the thirteen findings, resolved structurally

With the kit single-sourced, build.js check reads the same canonical tables and logic definitions the kit is built from, instead of reimplementing beat accumulation and anchor resolution from memory of them. The open findings close as a class. This is also the natural moment to decide TypeScript for the harness and the canonical source (D7): the seams are being written once anyway, and types there are cheap; types anywhere else wait.

path in: normally through REP1, because the canonical source must exist before check can read it; through the stall trigger otherwise, so the findings class never stays open on a plan's credit.

done if: the divergence cases the review's postmortem names (a missing size, empty SHOTS, subject: [], prototype-named beats) each resolve identically in check and in a driven page. trigger: if REP1 stalls for more than a working week, build the comparison bracket from forward item 5 instead. The class must not stay open on the strength of a plan.

REP3

Close the open bags

STYLE and CONFIG get a key registry derived from the kit's actual reads. The enumeration in breakdown.md was already produced mechanically, and that derivation becomes the registry's source, never a hand-list. check warns on unknown keys; film-private keys get an explicit marker.

path in: independent of REP1 and REP2 — it needs only the mechanical key-read enumeration re-run as a derivation, so it can start any time; its one risk is the registry decaying into a hand-list, which the refuted-if exists to catch.

done if: a misspelled kit key warns and names the near-miss, every shipped scene is warning-clean after annotation, and the registry is derived or controlled. refuted if: the registry cannot be derived from the reads. Then it is another copy of the code, and D5 is wrong as specified.

REP4

The extent fork, implemented

Whichever way REP0 decided. A wires probe's measurement against declared extents, inside its existing exception: read-only, authoring-time, in no artifact pipeline. B ships derivation helpers plus the hybrid rule for intent terms. The plan's recorded evidence (three of five wrong when hand-declared) is the prior. Either way the layer's most expensive gap finally has an instrument, or stops being representable.

path in: independent of the other phases — the first edit is to the corpus fixture (plant a deliberately wrong extent, watch the mechanism go red), and only then do the derivation helpers and the named-term rule reach shipped scenes.

done if: the corpus fixture gains a deliberately wrong extent and the chosen mechanism goes red on it before any shipped scene is touched. Red before green, on the corpus, as designed.

REP5

Instrument the movement triggers

The plan concludes that every promotion trigger is unfireable without a count, and nobody was counting. This phase makes the pattern-ledger's counting cheap enough that triggers actually fire. TRAVEL is the first candidate watched: the day a second film hand-rolls a travel closed-form, the count says so.

path in: deliberately last of the near-term phases — it needs signal worth counting (roughly two more films' worth) before instrumentation pays, and it fires early only if a migration stalls for want of a count.

done if: a migration proposal arrives with its trigger evidence and its named second consumer attached, without a human going looking. refuted if: two more films ship and nothing recurs or blocks. Then the residue really is residue, and the line already sits in the right place.

REP6

Longer term, VISION-gated

The derived-data tier takes its first large occupant: the physics bake, under the proposal's red lines, possibly with wasm compute behind the data boundary (D7). Presentation-time separation makes viewing speed declarative. state widens by named parameters as drivers arrive. None of this is scheduled. It is what the boundary was drawn to survive.

What this page does not deliver

  • Artifact independence for film declarations. VISION's stronger criterion, that a declaration can be read, validated, or rendered independently of the artifact embedding it, is not met by REP0 through REP6. REP1 emits the kit; the film tables stay embedded. That is agenda item 2, the source-artifact question, deferred rather than answered, and the site's source-equals-artifact messaging is one reason to defer it deliberately.
  • Determinism magnitude. VISION's other admitted-failing criterion, that a determinism failure reports its size and location, is out of this roadmap's scope. Noting it here so the roadmap does not read as covering both of VISION's open failures when it addresses only the extent gap.
  • A representation syntax. The settled table says no DSL, and the owner's 2026-07-30 position says JSON is not the right shape. This page draws the boundary; it does not pick a shape for the data side.

Wrong-if

  • The kit/film cost split fails at other carriers: the 0.4% declaration share is measured on one 3D carrier, and a materially different composition elsewhere would reopen "two separable programs."
  • The decidability test cannot sort a full scene inventory. A test that admits everything or nothing is not a boundary.
  • The shipped holes are unrepresentative: if the next film needs holes whose surroundings are not decidable without calling them, D1's condition fails as a rule.
  • The movement triggers are too slow for the actual strategic intent: if a deliberate one-time reframe of geometry and motion is the aim, D2 has miscast a strategy question as process.
  • "Render data as code" is about the input side, the film's source material as the data and the scene as its rendering. The site weakens this reading by declaring "there is no input format" as a design position, but the site settles nothing, so the wrong-if stays listed.
