## The squint measurement, which is the day's real lesson

Deciding whether canvas could replace ffmpeg for the squint strip took **three
metrics, two of them wrong**, and both wrong ones produced clean plausible
tables:

1. **PSNR** — measured fidelity to a reference, not the property in use. Said
   canvas was 10 dB worse and implied a problem that was not there.
2. **Sobel edge energy** — measured edge magnitude, cannot separate a crisp edge
   from a jagged one. Ranked the *aliased* image first, and I nearly shipped a
   decision on it.
3. **Intermediate-tone fraction on edges** — separates the two failure modes this
   instrument actually has. Only this one could answer.

**A metric that cannot separate your failure modes will still confidently rank
them.**

And the review side: four independent readers, one question with objective
ground truth, four answers. One right; a majority vote wrong. But **all four**
caught the composition difference. Coarse structural observations converge; fine
per-pixel judgments do not. Ask outside readers what they can SEE, not what you
can COMPUTE.

Worst moment: I sent a broken difference image and two readers used it to confirm
each other. Agreement between independent reviewers is worth nothing when they
share an input none of them can re-derive.

## The mistake worth keeping

**I reported clause 1 "closed" from a local run.** The clause says exercised
**in CI**. `gate.yml` fires on main/PRs/dispatch only, so every run on this
branch was `static` — which globs `scripts/` while `bracket-commands.js` lives
in `templates/`. The review verbs had never executed in CI at all, before or
after the migration. **That is the inference the item's own correction says to
stop making, made again one screen below where it is written down.** The owner
authorised a dispatch; it cost five minutes and produced the real evidence.

The pattern across this whole session: **writing the check is not the
measurement.** It caught something in nearly every unit of work —

- a `says` regex that could not cross a newline (exit code right, message wrong)
- a comment naming `set -e` as the guard when `if ! bun run` is the guard
- a Map audit that found five more misses than the review reported
- a bracket arm that decayed with the progress it measured (`poster` migrated
  out of the budget the arm used as its example)
- **R4.1's own baseline: recorded as one warning, actually four**

That last one is the sharpest. The gate for R4.1 is "byte-unchanged verdicts", so
a refactor checked against the wrong baseline would have passed while the
instrument went quieter — this repo's recurring failure, sitting inside the
baseline the gate was to be measured against.
